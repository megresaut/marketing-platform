01 — Overview

What This Platform Does

The Marex Sales Intelligence Platform is a proactive outreach and pipeline management system for Marex Boats USA. Where a standard CRM manages relationships that already exist, this platform manufactures the preconditions for them — surfacing qualified prospects before they walk into a competitor's booth, scoring them by replacement urgency, and identifying the shared yacht club affiliations that turn a cold call into a warm introduction.

The core loop: Ingest vessel ownership and market activity data → identify owners of aging competitor vessels with upgrade profiles that match a Marex model → score by urgency (listing activity, price reductions, ownership transfers) → surface shared yacht club affiliations with existing Marex clients → route to manual outreach with model-specific talking points, or into an automated sequence.

The platform does not require cold outreach from scratch. Every lead enters the system with context: the vessel they own today, the marina they use, the yacht clubs they belong to, and any overlapping affiliations with Marex's current client base. By the time a sales rep makes contact, they know more about the prospect's situation than the prospect would expect.

02 — The Problem

Why This Market Needs a Dedicated Tool

The luxury vessel replacement market is invisible by default. A Princess 68 owner deciding to upgrade to a Marex 460 does not announce the decision. They list their boat on YachtWorld. They reduce the price three weeks later. They ask around at the yacht club. By the time a dealer notices the listing and makes contact, two or three competitors have already reached them through their own networks.

General marine CRMs — MarineMax's internal tools, Salesforce with marine plugins — are structured around deals in progress, not deal creation. They have no mechanism for monitoring public vessel activity as a prospecting signal, no connection graph that routes introductions through shared club memberships, and no boat show pipeline that captures and scores on-dock contacts in real time.

The specific gap this fills: Galeon, Princess, Azimut, and Sunseeker owners in the 40–65 ft range, vessels 8–14 years old, concentrated in CT/NY, South FL, Chesapeake Bay, and the Great Lakes — approximately 2,400–3,200 qualifying vessels in the US market. This is the addressable replacement pool for Marex 375 through 460. None of them are reachable through passive inbound channels.
03 — Data Sources

Where the Intelligence Comes From

All core data sources in the platform are either public records or commercially available feeds with well-documented APIs. No scraping of protected systems. No paid data dependencies required for the demo — all demo content is pre-embedded from real public records.

USCG NVDC
Coast Guard National Vessel Documentation Center — documented vessel ownership records, HIN numbers, transfer history, and registered owner name/address. Public database. Queryable by vessel name, owner name, or official number. Primary ownership source for documented vessels ≥5 GT.
State Vessel Registration
State-level vessel registration records (FL DHSMV, CT DMV, NY DMV, IL DNR, TX PWD). Annual renewal creates a natural outreach cadence signal — registration window is also when owners reassess their current vessel. Covers undocumented vessels and dinghies/tenders.
YachtWorld / Boat Trader
Public vessel listing feeds — the primary trade-up signal. When a prospect's current vessel appears for sale (or drops in price), the platform triggers an immediate high-priority alert. Price reduction velocity is a strong urgency indicator. Listings include make/model/year/price/broker — enough to match against the prospect record.
IRS Form 990 (ProPublica API)
Nonprofit board membership, officer, and key employee disclosures. Source of yacht club board memberships — American Yacht Club, NYYC, Cruising Club of America, Chesapeake Bay Yacht Club, and others file 990s annually. Free public API — no API key required.
SEC EDGAR (Full-Text Search)
Corporate executive disclosures, proxy statements, 8-K officer appointments. High-net-worth vessel owners frequently appear in public company filings. Surfaces professional context and warm-introduction pathways via mutual directorships. Free public API — rate-limited but sufficient for prospect enrichment.
Marina Records
Dock lease agreements and slip registrations — where available publicly or through marina partnerships. New slip lease registrations signal active ownership; renewal windows are outreach touchpoints. Availability varies by state and marina management system.
NMMA Boat Show Data
Show attendee and exhibitor records. In production: per-show lead capture via unique QR/URL → direct pipeline entry with show-specific UTM tagging. Enables post-show scoring and follow-up sequencing. Demo uses per-show static lead capture links.

05 — Marine-Specific Refinements

What Makes This Different from a Generic Marine CRM

These are the ideas developed specifically for the Marex use case — refinements that have no equivalent in off-the-shelf marine CRM products and that reflect how high-net-worth yacht owners actually behave.

01
Trade-Up Signal Detection
When a prospect's current vessel appears on YachtWorld or Boat Trader, the platform triggers an immediate high-priority alert. Price reduction velocity adds urgency score — a 10–15% drop in 3 weeks means the owner is motivated and receptive. This is the highest-precision buying signal available in the market.
Source: YachtWorld + Boat Trader listing feed
02
Yacht Club Affiliation as Introduction Routing
IRS Form 990 board membership data is not just a display feature in the connection map — it informs the outreach talking points. When a prospect shares a board role at the American Yacht Club with an existing Marex client, the outreach email template references the club and proposes an introduction. The 990 source and EIN are displayed so the rep can verify the connection independently.
Source: ProPublica Nonprofit API · IRS Form 990
03
Model Fit Scoring
Each prospect's current vessel (make, model, length, year) is mapped to the most likely Marex replacement: Galeon 420 → Marex 440 GS; Princess 68 → Marex 460 SC; Riviera 5400 → Marex 440; Viking 62 → Marex 460. This surfaces automatically in the lead card and pre-populates the outreach talking points with model-specific specs and value propositions.
Logic: vessel length/type/brand → Marex model matrix
04
Marina-Level Lead Clustering
Prospects are clustered by marina, not ZIP code. A marina is a social and commercial community — three Galeon owners slipped at the same marina are aware of each other and talk. A rep who works one relationship well is visible to the adjacent prospects. The Market Map groups leads by marina cluster, shows cluster score, and flags when a marina has both a current Marex client and one or more prospects.
Source: USCG NVDC home port · state registration · marina slip records
05
Competitor Vessel Brand Migration Talking Points
The platform distinguishes prospects by their current brand: Galeon (Nordic Boats competitor — existing relationship leverage), Princess / Sunseeker (British semi-custom owners often moving to more utilitarian Scandinavian builds for different use cases), Azimut / Pershing (Italian sportboat buyers — different buying culture, price sensitivity), Viking / Hatteras (sport fishing segment, different use case and deck configuration priorities). Each brand cluster has a distinct outreach angle in the talking points engine.
Flag: flag-competitor · Brand-specific talking point templates
06
Marine LLC Entity Resolution
Luxury vessels over $500K are frequently owned by holding LLCs — "Osprey Point LLC," "Harbor Isle Holdings," etc. — a practice driven by liability insulation, state tax planning, and family estate structuring. The platform detects entity names and flags them for beneficial owner resolution via USCG NVDC registered agent records and state secretary of state filings. The resolved individual name drives the outreach and affiliation matching.
Flag: flag-entity · Source: USCG NVDC · State SOS business filings
07
Annual Registration Renewal as Outreach Window
State vessel registration renewals create a known annual calendar of prospect touchpoints. Florida registrations expire in December; Connecticut in March; New York in September. The platform tracks registration states for all prospects and queues outreach in the 30-day window before renewal — the moment an owner is most likely to reassess their current vessel investment.
Source: State vessel registration records · Calendar-driven re-engagement queue
08
Boat Show Lead Capture Link System
Each boat show in the pipeline generates a unique lead capture URL — short enough to display on dock signage, print on event badges, and share in show directory listings. Visitors who follow the link enter the prospect pipeline immediately with a show-specific source tag. Post-show, the pipeline view shows every contact captured at that show, their current vessel, and their model interest. The follow-up email sequence is triggered automatically 48 hours after show close.
Per-show QR + URL · UTM tagging · Automated 48h follow-up sequence

07 — Next Steps

From Demo to Live System

The demo establishes the interface and the intelligence model. Moving to a production system requires four infrastructure decisions, all of which have known, well-documented solutions:

Data Refresh Pipeline
Replace embedded demo data with a weekly automated pull from USCG NVDC, YachtWorld/Boat Trader listing feeds, and ProPublica 990 API. A lightweight backend job (Node/Python, 200–400 lines) writes updated JSON to a CDN-hosted file the front end reads on load. No database required for a single-market deployment.
Skip Tracing / Contact Data
Owner phone/email not available from public vessel records. BatchData or Melissa Data skip-tracing APIs return contact info for ~85% of confirmed individual owners. Entity-owned vessels require additional resolution before skip tracing. Pricing: approximately $0.08–0.12 per record, run on leads that score above threshold.
Email Delivery
Outreach sequences in the demo are simulated. Production deployment requires a transactional email provider (Postmark, SendGrid, or Brevo) and a sending domain authenticated for Marex Boats USA. Estimated sending volume: 200–500 prospect emails/month for a single-market build.
Hosting & Access Control
The current single-file build can be deployed to Vercel, Netlify, or any static host in under 10 minutes. For team access with login, a minimal auth layer (Magic Link / Clerk) can be added without restructuring the front end. No backend infrastructure required at this stage.
